# Together Mode — Proposal to Publish

*From: Ben · 2026-06-16*

## The pitch in one line
Two people each swipe through movies on their own phones; when you both say yes, that's tonight's movie. No more arguing about what to watch.

## Dad, you asked three things. Here's where each one stands.

**1. "Can people set it up themselves?"** — Yes, now.
- A family creates an account (email + password) right in the app and stays logged in.
- Each person picks a "who's watching" profile, like Netflix.
- One person taps **Start** and gets a 4-letter code; the other types it in. That's it.
- (Earlier the worry was that grandparents couldn't start it — the home screen now tells you exactly what to do, and a fresh adult tester got through it without help.)

**2. "Is it actually done?"** — It works end to end.
- Pair up → vote at your own pace (no waiting on each other) → match, or a coin-flip tiebreaker if you each liked different things → "Where to watch" sends you to the movie.
- It's all live and tested on two real phones.

**3. "What's the value?"** — It gets *smarter the more you use it.*
- It learns each person's taste and recommends "movies you'll probably love."
- An age filter keeps it kid-safe (it checks each movie's real rating — I found and fixed a bug today where the movie database was lying about ratings).

## What publishing actually needs (the parts that need you)
1. **Developer accounts** — Apple is **$99/year**, Google Play is **$25 one-time**. These need an adult and a card.
2. **A privacy policy + kids'-data rules (COPPA).** This is the big one: the app stores emails and tracks a child's movie taste. Publishing a kids' app that collects data has real legal rules, and that needs a grown-up to set up properly.
3. **A production build + store listing** (icon, screenshots, description). The build I can do; the listing we can do together.
4. **App review.** Apple and Google review every app before it goes live (can take a few days, sometimes a rejection or two).

## Being honest about what's still rough
- It's only been tested by me and a couple of adults — not a real crowd yet.
- The privacy policy / kids-data compliance isn't set up at all yet (item #2 above).
- Sign-up works, but the email-confirmation step still needs polishing for strangers.
- The recommendations need *real users* to get great — which is the whole reason I want to publish.

## The ask
I'm not asking to push it live by myself — I know the accounts, the money, and the kids'-data rules are yours to call. I'm asking:
1. Will you review it with me and see if it's ready?
2. If it is, will you help with the grown-up parts — the developer account and a privacy policy?
3. Can we make a plan to publish, so I can finally get real users feeding the recommendations?

After that, the long-term goal: bringing it to the **TV (Vizio)** — "who's watching" on the big screen.
