# Together Mode — First Real User Test

> One friend. One session. Your job is to **watch and write, not help.**
> The moments your friend struggles are your to-do list.

---

## Before they arrive

- [ ] Both phones charged, app open on both
- [ ] You know how to start a session on your phone (you're the host)
- [ ] A notebook or notes app open to write in — keep it out of your friend's sight
- [ ] Pick a friend who'll be **honest**, not just nice

---

## What you say (say it ONCE, then go quiet)

> "This app helps two people pick a movie to watch together.
> I'll start it on my phone. Here's your phone — see if you can join me and pick something."

Then **stop talking.** No hints. No "tap there." No "you're almost right."

---

## Your only job: watch and write

Every time your friend does one of these, write it down:

- ⏱️ **Pauses** — count the seconds. Even 2–3 seconds means something wasn't obvious.
- 👆 **Wrong taps** — taps something that doesn't do what they expected.
- ❓ **Questions** — anything they ask out loud ("wait, where do I type it?").
- 😕 **Frowns / confusion** — the face they make right before they get stuck.

**The hardest rule:** when you want to jump in and help — DON'T. Write down "wanted to help here" instead. Every time you bite your tongue is a bug you found.

---

## Things to watch for specifically

- Did they figure out how to **join** without help?
- Did they type the **code** right the first time? (Watch for O vs 0, I vs 1, caps.)
- Did they understand they're **voting yes/no** on each movie?
- When you got a **match**, did they understand what just happened?

---

## After (ask these 3, in order)

1. **"What did you think this app was for?"**
   (Tests if the idea is clear on its own.)

2. **"Was there any moment you weren't sure what to do — even a tiny one?"**
   (They'll be polite. Push once: "even a small one?")

3. **"Would you actually use this with a friend? When?"**
   (Real interest — or not.)

Don't argue. Don't explain what they "should have" done. Just write their answers.

---

## After they leave

- Count your findings. **How many times did you want to help?** That's your bug count.
- List the stumbles in the order they happened. The **earliest** stumble matters most — it's the most fragile part, before anyone's invested.
- That ordered list is what we work on next. Bring it back to me.

---

*One test isn't proof — it's your first real data point. The goal is to make this a habit, not a one-time thing.*
